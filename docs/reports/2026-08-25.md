# SEO / Performance Analysis - wordl.ryxwaer.com

**Report date:** 2026-08-25
**Site:** https://wordl.ryxwaer.com/
**Stack:** Nuxt 4 (SSR) → OpenResty → Cloudflare → user
**Last deploy:** 2026-06-21T23:06Z (commit `df2e3b7`) - the app ran untouched for ~9 weeks
**Analysis window:** 210 days of GSC (2026-01-26 → 2026-08-23); 16 months of first-party usage logs (2025-04-30 → 2026-08-25)
**Data sources:** GSC Search Analytics API (`skills/gsc_longrange.py`), GSC Links report (read from the UI - the API does not expose it), the app's own `query_logs` collection (`skills/usage_report.py`), Cloudflare GraphQL analytics, live CDP measurement of Core Web Vitals, and direct `curl` of headers / sitemap / theme API.

## Executive summary

**The headline finding is that Google is not our traffic source, and never really was.**

The app serves a real, engaged, and *growing* audience - **6.4 unique visitors per day**, 4 540 solver runs from 860 distinct IPs over 16 months, with **August 2026 the highest-ever month for unique users**. Over the same period Google organic delivered **5 clicks in 28 days**, or 0.17/day. Google accounts for roughly **3 % of actual usage**.

Every previous report in this folder, including my own first pass at this one, measured the site exclusively through Search Console and concluded the product was failing. That conclusion was wrong. Search Console was measuring a channel that was never carrying the traffic.

| Channel (August 2026) | Volume | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Actual solver users (first-party logs) | ~6.4 unique IPs/day | 100 % |
| Google organic clicks (GSC) | ~0.17/day | **~3 %** |
| Everything else (unknown - not instrumented) | ~6.2/day | **~97 %** |

Three conclusions, in order of importance:

1. **We do not know where 97 % of our users come from**, because no referrer is logged (§3). This is now the single most important gap, and it is a ~5-line fix.
2. **We have zero external backlinks** (§4). That is the real explanation for the ranking plateau - not content, not speed, not schema.
3. **The product is genuinely healthy and improving.** Usage is at an all-time high, 77 % of runs contain real clues, 16 % of visitors return on multiple days (§3). Two small technical regressions are live (§6), and performance is excellent (§7).

### The critical gap: no referrer is captured

`query_logs` documents contain exactly six fields: `timestamp`, `query`, `ip`, `country`, `userAgent`, `isMobile`. **There is no referrer and no landing path.** So the channel delivering 97 % of our users is completely uninstrumented. Candidates - direct/bookmarked (the 16 % returning cohort), other search engines (Bing/Brave/DDG/Yandex - note Russia at 72 runs), or shares in Telegram/Facebook/Zalo groups where Binance is popular in Vietnam, Indonesia, and Pakistan - but this is guesswork, and it should not be.

With Piwik now removed, adding `referer` and the landing path to the existing log write is the highest-value change available. It is a few lines in `server/api/words.ts`, needs no third party, and would turn the central unknown of this report into a measured fact within a week.

**Not one external backlink.** This reframes §1 completely. A site with zero referring domains that still holds average position 4.5–4.8 on its core commercial terms is doing *remarkably well* on relevance alone. It also explains, better than any other hypothesis in this folder's history:

- why we plateau at position ~5 and cannot reach the top 3
- why the May freshness "honeymoon" decayed instead of consolidating
- why competitors with thinner tools but real link profiles outrank us
- why position degrades whenever we stop shipping - we have no authority floor to fall back on

Previous reports attributed the plateau to content thinness, sitemap freshness, and title framing. Those are real but second-order. **Zero links is the binding constraint**, and no amount of on-page work removes it.

The "Internal links: 1" figure is a reporting artifact of the tiny crawl footprint, not a bug - the rendered HTML has proper anchors (homepage → `/wodl` ×3, `/about` ×2; `/wodl` → `/` ×5). Worth noting though that `/wodl` passes *more* link equity to `/` than it receives back, which compounds §6.2.

## §5. Where the organic queries actually are

Bucketing the top 150 queries over 210 days:

| Intent bucket | Queries | Clicks | Impr | CTR | Weighted pos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solver intent** (`wodl solver`, …) | 11 | 57 | 2 893 | 1.97 % | **4.8** |
| **Navigational** (`wodl`, `wordl` alone) | 2 | 2 | 2 181 | **0.09 %** | 8.3 |
| **Answers / word-list** (`… account defense 6 letter words`) | **101** | 7 | 467 | 1.50 % | **12.8** |
| Other | 36 | 8 | 219 | 3.65 % | 7.1 |

Three things follow:

**We already won our target niche.** Position 4.8 on solver intent is a good ranking. It produced 57 clicks in seven months because the pool is only ~14 impressions/day.

**Half our impressions are junk.** `wodl` alone is 2 181 impressions at 0.09 % CTR - those people want the Binance game, not a third-party solver. Same shape by segment: USA 7 877 impressions → 6 clicks (0.08 %); desktop 13 628 → 35 clicks (0.26 %) versus mobile 6 558 → 129 clicks (1.97 %). **Mobile converts 7.6× better per impression.** Never optimise against impressions here; track mobile clicks.

**The theme long tail is where organic volume exists, and we are invisible in it.** 101 distinct queries name specific weekly themes (`binance wotd trifi superapp answers today`, `binance wodl account defense 7 letters`, `binance wodl meme rush answers today`). Binance mints a fresh batch every week. We rank at position 12.8 and get ~0 clicks, because our title reads "Binance WODL Solver - Today's Word of the Day Answer" while the user typed a theme name and a letter count. Competitors put the theme, the letter count, and the date in the title.

## §6. Two live regressions

### 6.1 Sitemap `lastmod` frozen for 9 weeks (confirmed bug)

```
<lastmod>2026-06-21T23:06:04Z</lastmod>   ← all three URLs, = deploy timestamp
<!-- XML Sitemap generated by @nuxtjs/sitemap v8.0.11 at 2026-08-25T17:06:19.193Z -->
```

The XML renders per request, but `lastmod` is 9 weeks stale on pages declaring `changefreq: daily`. GSC confirms: **2 warnings, 3 submitted, 0 indexed.**

**Root cause:** `nuxt.config.ts` puts the dates in `sitemap.urls`, which in `@nuxtjs/sitemap` v8 is a **build-time** source - the array is resolved once at build and baked in, so `new Date()` evaluates at deploy. The in-code comment claiming `cacheMaxAgeSeconds` makes `urls()` run per request is wrong; that option caches the *rendered* sitemap. This fix has now half-landed twice (see 2026-05-31 §5). The correct v8 pattern is a runtime source:

```ts
// server/api/__sitemap__/urls.ts
import { defineSitemapEventHandler } from '#imports'
import type { SitemapUrlInput } from '#sitemap/types'

export default defineSitemapEventHandler(() => {
  const now = new Date().toISOString()
  return [
    { loc: '/', changefreq: 'daily', priority: 1.0, lastmod: now },
    { loc: '/wodl', changefreq: 'daily', priority: 0.9, lastmod: now },
    { loc: '/about', changefreq: 'monthly', priority: 0.7, lastmod: now },
  ] satisfies SitemapUrlInput[]
})
```

```ts
// nuxt.config.ts - replace `urls` with `sources`
sitemap: {
  cacheMaxAgeSeconds: 600,
  sources: ['/api/__sitemap__/urls'],
  exclude: ['/log', '/policy', '/red-packet', '/debug'],
}
```

Honest impact: this is not a crawl blocker (all three URLs are indexed and were crawled within 5 days). Fix it because it is cheap and currently contradicts itself, not because it will move rankings.

| Page | 210-day clicks/impr/pos | Current 28-day |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | 145 / 17 386 / 7.6 | 5 / 649 / 11.7 |
| `/wodl` | 17 / 3 141 / 9.4 | **0 / 7 / 58.3** |
| `/about` | 3 / 187 / 12.6 | 0 / 7 / 20.3 |

`/wodl` is not deindexed (PASS, indexed, crawled 2026-08-20, self-canonical honored) - Google is choosing not to rank it. Query×page shows why:

| Query | `/` | `/wodl` |
|---|---|---|
| `wodl solver` | 25 clicks, 1 467 impr, pos 4.5 | 1 click, 340 impr, pos 7.8 |

Six queries split across both URLs and the homepage wins every material one. The pages are near-duplicates: both titles begin `"Binance WODL Solver - "`, both H1s are `"Binance WODL Solver"`, both descriptions open `"Free Binance WODL … solver"`, both carry `WebApplication` schema, and `/wodl`'s actual function is to link back to `/` three separate times. So it competes with the homepage and loses, while contributing nothing on the theme queries it is uniquely equipped to win (live theme in H1, full per-length word pool, FAQ + HowTo schema).

## §8. Strategic read

The previous framing across this folder - "we are failing at SEO, and the niche is too small" - was built on a single channel that turns out to carry 3 % of the traffic. The corrected picture is:

- **The product works and is growing.** All-time-high usage, 73 % engagement, 16 % return rate, zero maintenance for nine weeks.
- **Organic search is a rounding error**, and it is capped anyway: ~14 impressions/day of solver intent, where we already rank ~5th.
- **The growth channel is unknown and unmeasured.** 97 % of users arrive from somewhere we have never looked.
- **Zero backlinks is why organic will not improve** through on-page work.

That reorders the priorities completely:

**1. Instrument the referrer (do this first, this week).** Add `referer` and landing path to the `query_logs` write. Everything below is guesswork until we know what the 97 % channel is. If it turns out to be Bing or a Telegram share, the entire strategy changes - and we would never learn that from Search Console.

**2. Ship the §6 fixes.** Cheap, and one of them is actively lying to Google.

**3. Then decide on organic, informed by (1).** If the referrer data shows a real non-Google channel, invest there instead. If we still want organic growth, the only lever that matters is **links, not content** - one genuinely relevant mention (a crypto forum, a Binance-community wiki, a "best WODL tools" roundup) would do more than any amount of schema. Note this does not require abandoning the product principle: the calculator analogy holds, and one canonical page that always reflects the current week is not a content directory. Repurposing `/wodl` from a duplicate solver page into *the theme-answers page* (title leading with theme + letter counts, `WebApplication` schema dropped, one CTA instead of three) fixes §6.2 and attacks §5's long tail in the same change.

**What I would not do:** more on-page SEO work in the hope of moving organic. We have already won the queries available to us, and the constraint is links.

## Methodology / reproduce

- **GSC (210 days):** `.venv-skills/bin/python skills/gsc_longrange.py`. Daily series, 28d-vs-prior-28d totals, query / page / country / device / query×page, `sitemaps.list`, `urlInspection` for four URLs. `endDate = today − 2` for GSC lag.
- **GSC Links:** read from the Search Console UI; the Search Analytics API does not expose link data at all.
- **First-party usage:** `.venv-skills/bin/python skills/usage_report.py`, reading `DB_URI` from `.env`. Filters bot user agents, buckets by month, treats one IP-day as a session, and reports engagement depth, geography, and repeat visitors.
- **Cloudflare:** GraphQL `httpRequests1dGroups` on zone `dfeaf65fde850f346fe0832c8f51fed4`. `workers_list` is empty (not a Workers app). `httpRequestsAdaptiveGroups` with `refererHost` / `clientRequestPath` / `edgeResponseContentTypeName` is rejected on the free plan, and adaptive queries are capped at a 1-day range.
- **Core Web Vitals:** CDP `Runtime.evaluate` with buffered `PerformanceObserver` for `largest-contentful-paint`, `layout-shift`, `longtask`, plus Resource Timing by origin. Desktop viewport 1101×1192 @ DPR 0.8; mobile field data would need CrUX.
- **Live checks:** `curl` of headers, `/sitemap.xml`, `/robots.txt`, `/api/theme`, `/api/theme-words`, and rendered HTML of `/` and `/wodl` (title / meta / canonical / headings / JSON-LD / anchors).
- **Caveats:** an IP is not a person - carrier NAT understates and mobile IP churn overstates unique visitors, so treat 6.4/day as an order of magnitude, not a precise count. The channel-share table in the summary compares GSC clicks to unique IPs, which are different units; the conclusion holds because the gap is 1–2 orders of magnitude, not because the ratio is exact. GSC anonymises low-volume queries, so §5 buckets cover the top 150 queries. August covers 23 days.
