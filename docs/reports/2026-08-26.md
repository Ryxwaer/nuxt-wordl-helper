# Backlinks and promotion - competitive reality

**Report date:** 2026-08-26
**Site:** https://wordl.ryxwaer.com/
**Follows:** `docs/reports/2026-08-25.md`, which established that Google is ~3 % of usage and that we have zero external backlinks
**Data sources:** first acquisition data from the referrer instrumentation deployed 2026-08-25 21:15Z (commit `846a40b`); `userAgent` history across all 4 548 logged runs; live SERP survey of the queries we would need to win

---

## Executive summary

**We are not competing against other tools. We are competing against exchange blogs that publish the answers outright.**

A survey of the queries a WODL player actually types returns, in order: `bittime.com`, `blog.mexc.com`, `cryptotimes.io`, `bitrue.com`, `socialschmuck.com`, `earnenews.com`. Four of those are cryptocurrency exchanges publishing Binance's answers on their own blogs, updated daily, to capture Binance users mid-puzzle. MEXC and Bitrue are direct Binance competitors with domain authority we cannot approach.

This settles the strategy question from the previous report. The ranking plateau is not a content or schema problem, and it is not fixable by writing more pages: **an answer-list page cannot outrank an exchange's answer-list page**, and the searcher who lands there has their answer and never needs a solver.

Two consequences:

1. **Answer-intent search is unwinnable and should be abandoned as a goal.** This aligns with the product principle of being a tool, not a content directory - the principle was right for reasons beyond taste.
2. **Our wedge is the one thing the lists cannot do: narrow by clues.** A static list of 17 words does not help a player who has three grey letters and a green in position 3. That player is our user, and they are reachable where they already are, not through search.

Where that is, and what to do about it, is §6 and §7. The short version: Binance builds link-sharing into the game's own reward loop (§6), and the best single move available is one genuinely useful post on Binance Square, whose terms explicitly permit third-party links (§7).

## §1. First acquisition data: the referrer is genuinely absent

Overnight 2026-08-26, 00:06→00:47Z, 4 runs from 2 visitors:

| time (UTC) | country | device | source | clues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00:06:49 | North Macedonia | Chrome mobile | `direct` | none (opening click) |
| 00:07:18 | North Macedonia | Chrome mobile | `direct` | has `a,r,e`, not `t,d`, `a` 3rd |
| 00:19:19 | North Macedonia | Chrome mobile | `direct` | has `a,r`, not `l,n`, `a` 3rd |
| 00:47:37 | Algeria | Chrome mobile | `direct` | has `a,d,r,e`, `a` 3rd, `d` 4th |

Every run reported an empty `document.referrer`. The instrumentation is not at fault - it was verified on 2026-08-25 to record a referring host when one is present, so `direct` is a real observation.

Absence still narrows the field. It rules out Google, which would have sent `google.com`. It also rules out social and messaging in-app browsers: across **all 4 548 runs ever logged**, Android WebView accounts for 9 runs (0.2 %) and the Google Search App 18 (0.4 %). Traffic is standalone browsers - 57 % Chrome mobile, 25 % Safari, 13 % Chrome desktop.

Both IPs were new, with nothing previously logged from their entire /24, so these were not bookmark returns. New mobile visitors with no referrer, in Binance-heavy markets, is the signature of **a link shared in a chat**: Android drops the referrer when handing a link from an app to Chrome. That is consistent with WebView being near zero - people open the link in Chrome rather than the app's own browser.

Caveat: this is 4 runs from 2 people. It is a hypothesis, not a finding. What supports it is the 30-day country mix, which looks nothing like search traffic - North Macedonia 133 runs/49 IPs, Pakistan 80/20, Russia 51/10, Algeria 41/23, Indonesia 38/6. Concentrated, mobile, emerging-market, word-of-mouth-shaped. Algeria has appeared on 210 separate days.

**Incidental validation:** both visitors' clue sets resolve to words on Binance's official "Gen Z Investing" list for that week - `LEARN` for the first, `TRADE` for the second. The theme scraper and word pool are correct and current.

## §2. Two mechanisms, often confused

The chosen direction, "backlinks and community outreach", is two different things with different payoffs. Worth keeping separate when judging results.

| | SEO backlinks | Community distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Guide sites, forums, tool directories | Binance Square, Telegram/WhatsApp groups |
| Effect | Raises the ranking ceiling; compounds slowly | Direct traffic now; no SEO credit |
| Crawlable | Yes | No - chat links are invisible to Google |
| Fits our data | Weakly (search is 3 % of usage) | Strongly (matches the observed channel) |

Community distribution is where the audience demonstrably is. SEO backlinks matter because we have **zero**, which caps organic regardless of how good the pages are - but the payoff there is smaller and slower than the raw count suggests, because the queries above are owned by exchange blogs.

## §3. Realistic backlink targets

Sites that explain WODL without publishing answer lists have a reason to link to a solver, because it completes their guide rather than competing with it:

- `inthenearfuture.com/binance-word-of-the-day-wotd-complete-guide/` - a full guide with no solver
- `earnenews.com` - guide plus answers; the guide half could cite a tool
- Tool aggregators - `itoolverse.com` lists a competing WODL filter, so the category is one they index

Not worth pursuing: `blog.mexc.com`, `bitrue.com`, `bittime.com`. These are exchanges monetising Binance's game; they will not link to a third-party tool, and `socialschmuck.com` is running its own Binance referral code.

**Direct competitor:** `itoolverse.com/crypto/binance-wodl-advanced-filter` is the same product - position, included and excluded letters, 3-8 letter pool, click-to-copy. Worth watching, and worth knowing we are not the only tool.

## §4. What not to do

- **Do not build answer-list pages.** Unwinnable against exchange blogs, and it converts us into the thing we chose not to be.
- **Do not spam communities.** The channel that works is people recommending the tool to other players. A burst of promotional posts damages exactly that.
- **Do not chase Telegram links for SEO.** They drive traffic, but earn no ranking credit.

## §5. Already done, no action needed

The technical groundwork for link-sharing is complete and verified live on 2026-08-26: Open Graph and Twitter card tags are present and correct on every page, and `og-image.jpg` returns HTTP 200 at the correct 1200x630 (64 KB, on-brand). A shared link renders a proper card, which is what matters for a channel built on people pasting links into chats.

## §6. The mechanic that explains the traffic

Binance's own WOTD announcements describe how a player unlocks their **second game of the day**:

> Unlock the second WOTD game once the selected article is successfully shared on social media **and the shared link is clicked by a third party.**

This is the most useful thing found in this analysis. Binance has built link-sharing into the game's reward loop: to play twice, you must get a stranger to click a link you posted. The predictable result is groups - Telegram, WhatsApp, comment threads - where players exchange links and click each other's, because both sides need it.

That is a population of WODL players who share links as a matter of routine, gathered in places built for exactly that, and it is the most plausible origin of the referrer-less mobile traffic in §1. It also explains why WebView is 0.2 %: a link pasted in a chat and opened on Android goes to Chrome.

Caveat worth holding: those shares are *transactional*. People click to unlock a game, not because they wanted a tool. Presence in such a group is not the same as demand, and traffic from it may be low-intent. The relevant inference is narrower - **sharing is normalised in this audience**, so a genuinely useful link travels further here than it would elsewhere.

## §7. Promotion options, compared

| Option | Mechanism | Effort | Payoff | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Binance Square** | Traffic + indexed mention on binance.com | Low-med | **High** | Ban for repeat promo |
| WODL link-exchange groups | Direct traffic only | Low | Medium | Spam perception; low intent |
| Guide-site outreach | Real SEO backlinks | Medium | Low-med | Mostly ignored |
| Tool directories | Low-quality backlinks | Low | Low | Little value |
| Reddit / forums | Traffic + nofollow link | Medium | Medium | Self-promo rules |
| Short-form video | Traffic | High | Unknown | Costly to test |
| Do nothing; improve retention | Keeps existing users | Medium | Medium | Ignores growth |

### Binance Square - the best single bet

Binance's Community Platform Terms state plainly, in the clause listing what needs copyright permission:

> Content from other websites (subject to all applicable terms, **incorporating links to 3rd party websites as part of your content posted on Binance Square, is however permitted**).

So linking out is explicitly allowed. Three things make this the strongest option:

1. **The audience is exactly right.** Square is where Binance itself posts WOTD announcements, so the readers are players.
2. **It is a high-authority indexed domain.** Square posts live at `binance.com/en/square/post/…` and surface in search. Even if the link is `nofollow`, an indexed mention on binance.com is worth more than anything a small guide site can give.
3. **It is free and immediate**, unlike outreach that depends on someone replying.

The constraint is real, though. The guidelines forbid "the release of low-quality content or continuous release of advertisements", and separately forbid "**Force, induce or hire** other users to … click on a link" - so no incentivised clicking. This has to be genuinely useful writing that happens to contain a link, published occasionally. One good post explaining how to narrow a WODL guess from partial clues, with the tool as the worked example, fits the rules. A weekly promo drop gets the account restricted.

Note also that posting invitations to Telegram or Discord groups is prohibited on Square, so the two community options cannot be combined.

### Guide-site outreach - the only real SEO lever

Worth doing because we are at **zero backlinks**, which caps organic regardless of page quality. Targets are guides that explain WODL without publishing answer lists, since a solver completes their page rather than competing with it: `inthenearfuture.com`, and the guide half of `earnenews.com`. Expect a low reply rate; this is a handful of links over months, not a campaign. Exchange blogs (`blog.mexc.com`, `bitrue.com`, `bittime.com`) are not worth contacting - they monetise Binance's game themselves.

### The option worth taking seriously instead

Per the 2026-08-25 report, 16 % of visitors already return on multiple days and 77 % of runs contain real clues. That is a tool people come back to. Spending the same effort on the returning-user experience may beat any acquisition channel here, especially since the addressable search market is owned by exchange blogs. This is not the exciting answer, but it should not be dismissed by default.

### Recommendation

**One well-written Binance Square post, then measure.** It is the only option that is simultaneously on-audience, free, immediate, permitted by the platform's own terms, and capable of producing a high-authority indexed mention. The referrer instrumentation now deployed will attribute it precisely - traffic arriving from Square will log `source: binance.com` rather than `direct`, so for the first time we will be able to tell whether a channel works instead of guessing.

Run guide-site outreach in parallel, since it is slow and independent. Hold off on video and directories until something proves it can move the number.
